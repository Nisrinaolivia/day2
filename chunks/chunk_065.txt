But in streamlining and being economical with language, the drafters of the new PS have
jettisoned much of what was useful in the old PS – including its emphasis on the diversity of mediation practice and its focus
on the centrality of self-determination. The new PS do not “specify clearly the minimum practice and competency requirements
for mediators” as recently claimed by the new chair of the MSB. For all the reasons discussed in this article, it is submitted that
this claim is overstated. There are significant gaps and uncertainties in the new PS. If the PS are to reach their full potential, the
gaps need to be filled, some sections need to be re-written in more user-friendly terms, and some guidance needs to be provided
in respect of a number of ethical dilemmas. The MSB, NMABs and individual mediators each have a part to play in ensuring that all relevant information relating to
mediation is provided to prospective participants, participants and anyone else contemplating the practice of mediation in
Australia. Information may be disseminated through a combination of supplementary guidelines and commentaries such as the
one provided in this article (from the MSB and NMABs), and information kits and agreements to mediate (steps which can
be taken by individual mediators). Eventually the MSB might revise the PS again to take into account the matters raised in
this article. Footnotes
