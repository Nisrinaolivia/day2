New PS s 7.5
Again, s 7.5 of the new PS is almost identical to s 9.2 of the old PS except that the “will” has become a “must”. Participants must be given “appropriate opportunities to speak to and be heard by one another” and to articulate their
needs, interests and issues. A mediator cannot guarantee that one participant will “hear” the other in the true sense of the
term but the mediator can create an environment and establish ground rules which will maximise the possibility of the
participants hearing, listening to and understanding each other. Mediators usually set three behavioural guidelines for the
conduct of mediation. These are that the parties do not interrupt each other, do not denigrate each other, and treat each
other in a respectful manner. 38 Most mediators will intervene to correct inappropriate behaviour on the part of participants
such as: attempting to cross-examine or interrogate their opponent; not allowing their opponent to speak freely; making
threats; or putting their opponent down, eg by name-calling.
