The
following matters need to be clarified:
• Mediators should help participants to get access to the standards (compare s 3.2(d) of the new PS with s 3.6 of the old PS). • Mediators need to be alert to pre-existing power imbalances, as well as to changes in the balance that may occur during
mediation (see s 6.1 of the new PS). • The wording of s 7.1, which gives a partial definition of “impartial”, might be amended to include bias by “word” as well
as bias by “action” and to extend the provision to a perception of bias, as well as actual bias. • The additional criteria listed in s 9.7 of the old PS (for assessing any proposed agreement) might be added to s 7.7 of the new
PS. These are important criteria which highlight two of mediation's strongest features – it can produce outcomes that are
different from those which might be delivered by a court; and it can produce outcomes which the parties consider to be fair. • The new PS are said to set minimum standards, yet the lists of knowledge, skills and ethical principles in s 10 appear to be
exhaustive and closed. In the coming years, other competencies might be added to these lists. For example, all mediators
(and all lawyers) need a basic knowledge and understanding of psychology. There are gaps in the new PS of which mediators should be mindful. These are discussed next. 144
Mind the gaps
The new PS have left gaps which did not exist in the old PS. The most significant gaps are discussed below. Application of the standards
It is a concern that the new PS might only apply to mediators who adopt a facilitative model of mediation.
