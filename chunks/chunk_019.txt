The new PS do not contain any provisions equivalent to s 3.4 of the old PS and as a result, several important provisions
have been lost. They are:
1. Section 3.4 required the mediator to “describe and explain the mediation process that is to be used”. This
sounds as though the mediator had to explain his or her own particular approach rather than providing a
general “description of mediation and the steps involved” (s 3.1(a) of the new PS). 2. The mediator had to discuss the appropriateness of mediation, its benefits and risks, and the alternatives
open to the particular participants in the particular circumstances. Again, this is more context specific than s
3.2(a) of the new PS. 3. Perhaps most important of all, s 3.4(c) of the old PS has not been reproduced in the new PS. This provision,
which relates to confidentiality, is discussed further below. 4. Mediators were required to “advise participants of the mediator's role in relation to the provision of advice
or other services” (eg a mediator might say, “although I am a lawyer, my role is not to give you legal
advice”). 121
Many mediators will be trained to deal with these matters as part of their opening statement and they will do so
instinctively.
