The PS should be another source of the duty – the PS should provide that mediators owe the
participants a duty of confidentiality in regard to information arising in connection with the mediation subject to stipulated
exceptions. Second, for the reasons discussed above (see the discussion on ss 9.1 and 3.2(b) of the new PS), mediators should
be required to personally explain to the participants, before commencing the mediation, the requirements of confidentiality and
its exceptions. It is an unsafe practice for mediators to rely on intake officers to explain these matters (and in any event, the
PS do not explicitly require intake officers to explain about the exceptions to confidentiality). Third, the standards should offer
some guidance to mediators who are subpoenaed or otherwise required to testify or produce documents in court. The new PS
are silent on this matter. Accountability, professional conduct and integrity
Absence of clear definitions of terms such as “good faith” and “cooperation” is problematic. It is not a new problem. The new
PS have introduced more uncertain terms, eg what do the terms “professional conduct” and “integrity” mean and require from
a mediator? If mediators are accountable, to whom are they accountable and for what? How does integrity differ from honesty,
if at all? Mediators orchestrate the flow of communication in mediation. They are skilled in directing the flow of communication using
techniques such as reframing and questioning.
