Provisions omitted from old PS s 1
There are a number of important provisions that have been jettisoned from s 1.1 of the old PS. These provisions
communicated to all stakeholders, from the very first paragraph, a clear statement as to who the PS applied to, a definition
of mediation, a sense of the purpose of mediation and of the role of a mediator, all conveyed in easy to understand terms
and without any need to cross-reference to other sections. The new PS do deal with the role of a mediator, both in Part
I and also in ss 2.1 and 2.2, but the terms used might be difficult for some people to understand (see the discussion
pertaining to s 2). Section 1.1 of the old PS clearly specified the relationships the PS were intended to govern. Again, this provision has been
whittled down and the new PS appear only to govern the relationship between mediators and participants, though there is
some mention of “others” in s 1.2(b) and reference to colleagues in s 8. However, there is no sense that the new PS also
govern the mediator's relationship to courts and tribunals, and to the general public. On this point, the provision (the last
sentence) from s 1.1 of the old PS is to be preferred. 114
Section 1.1 of the old PS mentions that mediation can be used “to form a future plan of action” and to make decisions
“about future actions and outcomes”. These references have been omitted from the new PS. (While mediation is described
as a decision-making process in ss 2.1 and 2.2 of the new PS, there is no mention of the making of decisions about future
plans, actions and outcomes.) As a result, two important points have been lost from the old PS. First, mediation is not just a
dispute resolution process. It can be used for other purposes, such as to negotiate the terms of an agreement between parties
who have never dealt with each other before.
