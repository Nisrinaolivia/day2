First, mediators must be made aware of the various objectives and values claimed for mediation. The relevant objectives
include: 65 dispute resolution according to standards agreed by the parties, satisfaction of individual interests or needs, party
empowerment, mutually acceptable outcomes which are also fair and stable, outcomes which represent the best alternatives
available (or Pareto optimal solutions) and maximisation of joint gains, enhanced relationships (or at least, minimisation of
damage to relationships), increased access to a “higher quality justice” (ie justice that is responsive to individual needs and
reflective of the preferences of the parties), efficiency and effectiveness. The values of mediation include:66 party participation
and autonomy; self-determination; informed consent; process fairness; and mediator impartiality. Failure to prioritise various objectives and values
The PS do not indicate which objective and value, if any, is paramount in the event of conflict (eg in the way that standards
for lawyers prioritise the duty owed to the courts and the administration of justice).
