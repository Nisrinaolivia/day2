(s 9.2). There is another minor change of note: s 6.4 of the old PS appeared to give participants the opportunity to discuss and
agree upon the obligation of confidentiality attached to separate sessions. Section 9.2 of the new PS appears to give the
mediator the power to decide on the issue. It is possible that a mediator might say “nothing will be confidential unless
you tell me it is to be treated as confidential” or “the only thing that is confidential is this …” Given that one of the stated
objectives of the PS is to achieve some level of consistency, it might have been better to regard everything said or done in a
separate session as confidential subject to specified exceptions. 136
New PS
10 Knowledge, skills and ethical principles
10.1: A mediator, consistent with the Approval Standards, must have the knowledge and skills, and an understanding of the
ethical principles, outlined below. Three separate lists are given, the first for knowledge, the second for skills and the third for ethical principles. The
expression “including, but not limited to” is not used at the beginning of the lists (as it was in s 7.3 of the old PS). The areas of knowledge listed in s 10.1(a) are much the same as those listed in s 7.3(a). Of note, item (x) has been changed
such that mediators are now required to have knowledge about “the law relevant to mediators and to the mediation
process” (cf s 7.3(a)(x) which required mediators to have knowledge relating to: “The law of mediation on confidentiality,
enforceability of mediated agreements and liability of mediators”). The terms of the new provision are wider. There have been some changes to the list of skills.
