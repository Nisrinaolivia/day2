mediators to conduct the mediation process in a procedurally fair manner. Additionally one of the competences required of
mediators under s 7.3(c) of the old PS was an ethical understanding in relation to (vi) “Supporting fairness and equity in
mediation”. A similar phrase has been retained in the list of ethical principles in s 10.1(c)(v) of the new PS. Neither “fairness” nor “equity” have been defined in either the old or the new PS. It is suggested that the obligations
arising from s 7.1 relate to procedural aspects of mediation, and not to the outcome of mediation. Support for this
conclusion can be found in the statement of principle from s 5 and from the terms of s 5 of the old PS which relate to the
manner in which mediation is conducted; and from the heading and statement of principle from s 9 of the old PS – in fact
almost every sub-section of s 9 spoke to the mediator's responsibility to conduct mediation “in a procedurally fair manner”. The same can be said of s 7 of the new PS: the section is headed “procedural fairness” (not outcome fairness), and the
obligations imposed by the section all relate to the manner in which mediation is conducted. 34 Mediators discharge their
obligations in relation to process fairness by, for example, ensuring that participants have an opportunity to speak and to be
heard; to obtain “independent professional advice or information” when needed; to take part in “balanced” negotiations in
which each participant has been treated in an even-handed and respectful manner.
