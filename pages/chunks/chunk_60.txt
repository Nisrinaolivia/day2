it (see, eg ss 2.3, 2.4(g) and 9.7 of the old PS which have been omitted). As has been noted above in the commentary, there are some provisions in the new PS which might be better worded. The
following matters need to be clarified:
• Mediators should help participants to get access to the standards (compare s 3.2(d) of the new PS with s 3.6 of the old PS). • Mediators need to be alert to pre-existing power imbalances, as well as to changes in the balance that may occur during
mediation (see s 6.1 of the new PS). • The wording of s 7.1, which gives a partial definition of “impartial”, might be amended to include bias by “word” as well
as bias by “action” and to extend the provision to a perception of bias, as well as actual bias. • The additional criteria listed in s 9.7 of the old PS (for assessing any proposed agreement) might be added to s 7.7 of the new
PS. These are important criteria which highlight two of mediation's strongest features – it can produce outcomes that are
different from those which might be delivered by a court; and it can produce outcomes which the parties consider to be fair.
