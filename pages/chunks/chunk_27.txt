Section 6.2 is adequate. However, there are no strategies offered to deal with two very common sources of power
imbalance: 1) when one party has access to more/better information and advice; and 2) when one party has access to
greater financial resources (the two power sources will often overlap). Mediators can provide participants with information
about other services and resources but this is of limited use in many circumstances. They cannot provide missing
information themselves without jeopardising their impartiality. Ultimately, a mediator may have no choice but to suspend
or terminate the mediation. Other provision omitted from old PS s 4
The statement of principle from s 4 of the old PS has been omitted. Since the training requirements (including training to
recognise and handle power imbalances) are canvassed in the separate NMAS Approval Standards, this omission does not
seem problematic. New PS
7 Procedural fairness and impartiality
7.1: “A mediator must conduct the mediation in a fair, equitable and impartial way, without favouritism or bias in act or
omission” (cf statement of principle from ss 5, 5.1 and statement of principle from s 9 of the old PS).
